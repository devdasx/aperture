Getting started
What is Aperture?+

A non‑custodial cryptocurrency wallet for iPhone and iPad. It holds Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, and twenty‑one more networks under a single 24‑word phrase, generates keys in your device's Secure Enclave, and is fully open source with a reproducible build. Non‑custodial means exactly what it says: you hold the keys — we never do.

Do I need to create an account?+

No — and you can't, because there isn't one. No email, no username, no onboarding form. You download the app, keys are generated on your device, and your wallet exists. That's the entire setup.

Is Aperture free? Are there hidden fees?+

The app is free and adds no fee of its own — ever. The only cost of a transaction is the network fee paid to the blockchain that processes it, and Aperture shows you that real fee before you sign. No spread, no markup, no premium tier.

Which networks are supported?+

Twenty‑four networks across five cryptographic families: Bitcoin, Bitcoin Cash, Litecoin, Dogecoin · Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, Optimism, Scroll, zkSync Era, Polygon, BNB Chain, opBNB, Avalanche, Celo · Solana, Stellar, Sui · XRP Ledger, Aptos, NEAR, Polkadot, TON, and TRON — plus tokens on the major ones. One phrase covers them all.

Is there an Android or desktop version?+

No. Aperture is built around Apple's Secure Enclave — dedicated key‑protection hardware that exists only on Apple devices — so it runs on iPhone and iPad (and natively on Apple‑silicon Macs as an iPad app). Porting it elsewhere would mean abandoning the security architecture the whole product stands on.

Can I import a wallet I already have?+

Yes — three ways: a recovery phrase (12 or 24 words, from any BIP‑39 wallet), a single private key, or a watch‑only import (an address or extended public key) that lets you monitor balances without the keys ever touching the device. Passphrase‑protected phrases are supported too.

Keys & security
Where are my private keys stored?+

On your device, and only there. Keys are generated on‑device with hardware entropy, protected by the Secure Enclave, and encrypted at rest with AES‑GCM‑256 under a device‑local master key. They are never transmitted to any server — Aperture holds no copy at any point.

What happens if I lose my iPhone?+

Your 24‑word recovery phrase is the recovery mechanism. Install Aperture on a new device, enter the phrase, and your wallets — every network — come back. The phrase is shown once during setup, client‑side only. Write it down and keep it offline; whoever holds it holds the funds.

I lost my recovery phrase. Can you recover my wallet?+

No — and it's important to understand that this is a feature, not a limitation. There is no reset flow, no support override, no backdoor, because no copy of your phrase exists anywhere but with you. If your phrase is lost and your device is gone, the funds are gone. We say this plainly before you ever create a wallet.

What if someone steals my phone?+

They face three walls: the device passcode, Face ID on the app itself, and Face ID again on every action that touches keys — sending, exporting, revealing the phrase. Keys stay encrypted the entire time. And because your phrase restores everything, you can move your funds to a new wallet from another device before a thief gets anywhere.

Why does Aperture ask for Face ID so often?+

Because a signature is money leaving. Every sensitive action is individually gated by biometrics, so an unlocked phone left on a table is not an open wallet. Biometric matching happens inside iOS and the Secure Enclave — Aperture only ever receives a yes or a no, never your face data.

Is the iCloud backup safe to use?+

It's optional, and it's engineered so it can't betray you: the phrase is encrypted on your device before upload — 600,000 rounds of PBKDF2 derive a key from your backup password, then AES‑GCM‑256 seals it. Only ciphertext reaches iCloud. Apple can't read it, we can't read it, and there is no copy of your password anywhere — lose the password and the backup is permanently unreadable. The app tells you this in exactly those words.

Aperture warned me about a phrase I tried to import. Why?+

The app ships with a built‑in list of publicly known phrases — BIP‑39 spec examples, Hardhat and Ganache developer defaults, seeds from tutorials and videos. Money on those addresses is swept by bots within seconds. If a phrase you import is on the list, Aperture warns you before you use it. The check runs entirely on‑device; nothing is looked up online.

What actually happens when I delete the app?+

Everything local is destroyed. iOS normally lets keychain entries survive deletion — so on the first launch after a re‑install, Aperture deliberately wipes every credential it ever stored, and a public test fails if any store is ever left out of that wipe. Delete means delete. Your funds, of course, remain on‑chain — restorable anywhere with your phrase.

Privacy
What data does Aperture collect about me?+

None. No identity, no age, no analytics, no crash reports, no advertising identifiers. There is no server for the app to report to and no account to attach data to — and the open‑source code lets anyone verify both claims. The full detail is in the Privacy Policy.

So is my activity completely invisible?+

Honestly: not completely — no wallet's is. To read balances and broadcast transactions, your device talks directly to third‑party blockchain infrastructure (Electrum servers, RPC providers, explorers, price feeds). Like any server on the internet, they can see your IP address and the addresses you query — never your keys, and never a name, because requests carry no identity. A VPN or iCloud Private Relay detaches your IP if you want that too. Most wallets don't tell you this; we'd rather you know.

Does Aperture ask for my age or identity?+

Never — there is nothing to attach it to. Per the Terms of Use, Aperture is intended for adults who have reached the age of majority in their jurisdiction; self‑custody is real responsibility. But no age, identity, or KYC data is ever requested or collected.

Sending & receiving
Can I cancel or reverse a transaction?+

No — blockchain transactions are irreversible once broadcast, by anyone, including us. That's why Aperture puts everything in front of you before you sign: the exact recipient, the amount, and the real network fee — and why it flags look‑alike "poisoned" addresses before they can trick you.

What is address poisoning, and how does Aperture help?+

Scammers send you dust from an address crafted to look like one you've used — same first and last characters — hoping you'll copy it from history later. Aperture checks recipients against this pattern and warns you before you send, when it still matters.

Why isn't my balance updating?+

Balances come straight from public blockchain infrastructure, and sometimes a third‑party server is slow, rate‑limited, or briefly down. Pull to refresh, or give it a moment — your funds live on the chain itself, not in the app, so a stale number is a display delay, never money at risk.

Does Aperture support ENS or other name services?+

Yes — you can send to human‑readable names: ENS names on Ethereum and SNS names on Solana resolve right in the send flow, with the resolved address shown for you to verify before signing.

Verification & trust
How do I verify the app myself?+

Clone the repository at github.com/devdasx/aperture, build it with the pinned toolchain following the reproducible‑build instructions, and compare your output with the App Store binary — they match byte for byte. The code you audited is provably the code you run. No other major multi‑chain mobile wallet offers this.

Who audits the code?+

Anyone who wants to — permanently, with no NDA and no permission needed. The codebase is kept deliberately auditable: two dependencies (Trust Wallet Core and GRDB, both open source), Swift 6 with strict concurrency, and a public test suite over the paths that matter — derivation, signing, storage, and reset.

How is Aperture different from MetaMask, Trust Wallet, or Rabby?+

Three things, together: coverage beyond EVM (Bitcoin and Solana sit beside Ethereum under one phrase, where Rabby is EVM‑only), Secure Enclave key protection at the hardware level, and a fully open‑source codebase with a reproducible build — a combination none of the major multi‑chain mobile wallets currently matches. MetaMask's mobile code isn't fully open; Trust Wallet ships no reproducible build.

I still have a question. Where do I ask it?+

The Contact page reaches the people who built the app — by email or a public GitHub issue. One rule above all: never share your recovery phrase, private keys, or PIN with anyone, including us. We will never ask.
