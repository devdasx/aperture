Security

Nothing to trust.
Everything to verify.

Aperture is built so you never have to take anyone's word for it — not even ours. Keys are born on your iPhone, every action is sealed behind Face ID, and every line of code is public.

Open source  ·  Reproducible builds  ·  No accounts

Born in silicon. Kept in silicon.

When you create a wallet, its keys are generated on your iPhone — entropy drawn from hardware, never from a server. They are encrypted the moment they exist, and they never leave. Aperture holds no copy. Nothing to hand over. Nothing to breach.

Secure Enclave
Key material is generated and protected by dedicated security hardware, physically isolated from the application processor — not by software promises.
AES‑GCM‑256
Every secret is sealed at rest with authenticated encryption under a device‑local 256‑bit master key. Tampered data simply refuses to open.
24 words, once
The only backup is yours: a BIP‑39 recovery phrase, shown once, client‑side only — never stored, never transmitted. Try to screenshot it and Aperture asks you to reconsider.

Aperture can't see your funds. That isn't a policy. It's the architecture.

No accounts. No analytics. Balances are read from public chains directly on your device. Nothing flows to us — there is no server for it to flow to.

Locked by Face ID.

Your face stands between the world and your keys — on every action, not just at the door.

Every signature

Sending, exporting a key, revealing a phrase — each demands biometrics first. No password to forget, phish, or reuse.

Auto‑lock

Step away and the shutter closes. Aperture locks itself and the wallet sleeps behind Face ID until you return.

A PIN, hashed

The fallback PIN is stored as a salted PBKDF2 hash with failure lockouts — never as the number itself.

Defense in depth.

Small guards, everywhere — the kind most wallets never think about.

It knows the seeds everyone knows.

Import a phrase from a tutorial — a Hardhat default, a BIP‑39 test vector — and Aperture warns you before a single coin lands on it. The blocklist ships inside the code; nothing is fetched.

Delete means delete.

Remove the app and the wallet is truly gone. On a fresh install, Aperture wipes every credential it ever owned — nothing quietly survives, nothing resurrects.

Poisoned addresses, caught.

Look‑alike recipients — the classic address‑poisoning trick — are flagged before you send, not explained after.

The real fee, before you sign.

Every transaction shows its true network cost up front. Honesty is a security feature: surprises are how people get moved to sign things.

A backup only you can open.

iCloud backup is optional — and useless to everyone but you. Your phrase is encrypted on the device before it goes anywhere. Only ciphertext, a random salt, and the work factor ever leave. Apple can't read it. We can't read it.

600,000

PBKDF2‑HMAC‑SHA256 iterations stand between your password and the key — an offline guess is made expensive by design.

AES‑GCM‑256

Authenticated encryption seals the phrase. A wrong password doesn't produce garbage — it simply refuses to open.

0

Copies of your password, anywhere. Lose it and the backup is permanently unreadable — Aperture tells you so, plainly.

Don't take our word for any of this.

Every line of Aperture is public. The App Store binary is reproducible from source, byte for byte — build it yourself and compare.
