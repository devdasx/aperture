Aperture Privacy Policy

Effective July 6, 2026  ·  Applies to Aperture for iOS, version 2.40  ·  Version history

In one sentence: Aperture collects no personal data — and this policy exists to explain precisely what that means, including the small amount of technical exposure that is inherent to using any blockchain from any app.

1. Scope

This policy covers the Aperture application for iOS and iPadOS ("Aperture", "the app"), a non‑custodial, open‑source cryptocurrency wallet published at aperturex.io with source code at github.com/devdasx/aperture. It describes what data the app handles, where that data lives, and what third parties are technically able to observe when the app talks to blockchain networks on your behalf.

2. Data Aperture collects: none

Aperture operates no servers of its own, requires no account, and contains no analytics, telemetry, crash‑reporting, or advertising code. The app has no mechanism to transmit information to us, and the open‑source code allows anyone to verify this. Specifically, we do not collect:

  • Identity and contact data. No name, email address, phone number, or username is ever requested — there is no sign‑up form in the app.
  • Age and demographic data. Your age, gender, location, and language habits are never asked for, inferred, or profiled.
  • Financial and wallet data. Your private keys, recovery phrase, addresses, balances, and transaction history are processed and stored on your device only. Aperture (the company and its developers) never receives them.
  • Usage and diagnostic data. No screen views, taps, session lengths, feature usage, crash logs, or performance metrics leave your device.
  • Identifiers. No advertising identifier, device fingerprint, or tracking cookie is read or created.

3. Data stored on your device

Everything the app needs lives in its private sandbox on your iPhone: your wallet database, encrypted key material, cached balances and prices, and your preferences. Secrets — recovery phrases, seeds, and imported private keys — are encrypted at rest with AES‑GCM‑256 under a device‑local master key. This data never syncs anywhere unless you explicitly enable the encrypted iCloud backup described in Section 7.

Deleting the app deletes this data. On the first launch after a re‑install, Aperture additionally wipes every credential it ever stored in the system keychain, so a deleted wallet cannot silently resurrect.

4. Device permissions

  • Face ID / Touch ID. Biometric evaluation is performed entirely by iOS and the Secure Enclave. Aperture receives only a yes‑or‑no answer; it never sees, stores, or transmits biometric data.
  • Camera. Used only to scan wallet‑address and payment QR codes. Frames are decoded on the device and are never stored or transmitted.
  • Photo library (add‑only). Used only when you tap Save to export a QR image of a key or recovery phrase. Aperture can add that one image; it cannot read your photo library.

5. Network requests to third‑party infrastructure

This is the honest part most wallet policies leave out. A wallet must talk to blockchains, and blockchains are served by infrastructure that Aperture does not operate. When the app reads your balances, fetches history, estimates fees, or broadcasts a transaction, your device connects directly — with no Aperture server in between — to third‑party services, which include:

  • Electrum servers for the Bitcoin family (Bitcoin, Bitcoin Cash, Litecoin, Dogecoin), operated by independent volunteers and companies.
  • JSON‑RPC providers for Ethereum, Solana, and the other supported networks — for example Infura, OneRPC, and public community endpoints.
  • Block explorers such as Etherscan and Tronscan, used to assemble transaction history.
  • Market‑price services, used to display fiat values for your assets.

Like any server on the internet, these services can technically observe: your IP address; the addresses and transactions you query; and the timing and frequency of requests. They cannot see your private keys, recovery phrase, PIN, or biometrics — signing happens on your device — and no request ever carries a name, account, or user identifier, because none exists. These providers act under their own privacy policies, not ours; Aperture receives nothing from them about you and has no side channel to correlate you across them.

If you want to detach even this technical exposure from your IP address, use a VPN or iCloud Private Relay. Every endpoint the app calls is listed in the open‑source code, so the full list can be audited at any time.

6. Blockchain transactions are public

When you send a transaction, it is broadcast to a public network and recorded permanently on a public ledger. Anyone can see the sending address, receiving address, amount, and time. This is a property of blockchains themselves, not of Aperture, and no wallet can undo it.

7. Optional iCloud backup

If — and only if — you enable it, Aperture can store a backup of your recovery phrase in your personal iCloud. The phrase is encrypted on your device before upload: a key is derived from your backup password using PBKDF2‑HMAC‑SHA256 with 600,000 iterations and a random salt, and the phrase is sealed with AES‑GCM‑256. Only the ciphertext, the salt, and the iteration count leave the device. Apple stores this blob under your iCloud account subject to Apple's terms; neither Apple nor Aperture can decrypt it. There is no recoverable copy of your backup password anywhere — if you lose it, the backup is permanently unreadable.

8. Children and age

Aperture collects no age data for the same reason it collects nothing else: there is no account to attach it to. The app is not directed at children, and self‑custody of funds is a serious responsibility — Aperture is intended for adults who have reached the age of majority in their jurisdiction (see the Terms of Use). Because no personal information is collected from anyone, no personal information is collected from children.

9. Your rights and choices

Privacy laws such as the GDPR and CCPA grant rights to access, correct, delete, and port personal data, and to opt out of its sale. Aperture holds no personal data about you, so there is nothing for us to disclose, correct, sell, or delete — those rights are satisfied by architecture rather than by request forms. To erase everything the app knows: delete the app (local data is destroyed) and, if you created one, delete the encrypted backup from Settings. We do not sell personal information; we have none to sell.

10. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will ship with the app and be published on this page. Every revision is version‑controlled in the public repository, so the complete history of what changed, when, and why is permanently auditable — the same standard we hold the code to.

11. Contact

Questions about this policy can be raised publicly — where answers benefit everyone — by opening an issue at github.com/devdasx/aperture, or via aperturex.io.
